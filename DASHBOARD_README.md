# Diagnostic Dashboard Guide (`DASHBOARD_README.md`)

This document explains how to read and interpret the per-species validation dashboards generated by **ValidateClassificationsSMK (v2)** for the 10-year Ljungbyhed airborne environmental DNA (eDNA) shotgun metagenomic time series (**516 weekly air-filter samples, 2013–2022**).

Every panel (`A` through `J`) is documented below in two complementary formats:
1. **Plain-English Guide**: Written for biologists, ecologists, and manuscript reviewers who were not involved in developing the pipeline.
2. **Technical & Mathematical Specification**: Exact equations, normalizations, statistical cutoffs, and the rationale behind each methodological choice.

---

## 1. Dashboard Variants & Parameter-Namespaced File Naming

Upstream Stages 0–3 (`00_ledger`, `01_inputs`, `02_classifiable_regions`) are shared across runs, while Stage 4 & 5 outputs are automatically namespaced by **`{map_tag}` = `mapq{min_mapq}_aln{min_align_pct}_id{min_identity_pct}`** (default: **`mapq30_aln80_id99`**) so runs with different mapping parameters never overwrite each other:

## 2. Figure Header & Color-Coded Validation Status Badges

At the top of every figure:
- **Main Title**: Shows the species scientific name in ***bold italics***, its NCBI Taxonomy ID (`TaxID`), and a rounded **status badge** colored identically to the merged HTML report.
- **Subtitle Line 1 (Reference & Read Summary)**: Lists the selected reference genome accession and total size, total **Classifiable Region (CR)** sequence length (`Mb` and `%` of genome), retained clean CR `%` across clean bins, total CR segment count, and eDNA read-pair retention (`raw` $\rightarrow$ `CR-mapped` $\rightarrow$ `clean`).
- **Subtitle Line 2 (Validation & Ecological Concordance Summary)**: Lists initial vs. final linear correlation ($R$), Spearman rank correlation ($\rho$), number of pruned outlier bins, 516-week linear signal retention ($R$), and 52-week seasonal phenology concordance ($r$).

### Panel A — Genomic Proportionality Regression
*Subtitle: `Mapped read pairs vs. classifiable region (CR) length per bin (top = contigs & median CR kb; whiskers = intra-bin IQR)`*

#### 1. Plain-English Guide
- **Core question**: *Do longer stretches of unique, species-specific reference DNA attract proportionally more environmental DNA reads?*
- If an organism is genuinely present in the air-filter samples, random shotgun sequencing acts like rain falling evenly across its genome: a genomic bin with twice as much **Classifiable Region (CR)** DNA should catch roughly twice as many reads.
- Each dot represents one of **100 genomic bins**, ordered from smallest (`bin_001` on the left, built from shorter contigs) to largest (`bin_100` on the right, built from longer contigs).
  - **Circles (`●`)** are **retained clean bins** that follow the expected linear relationship.
  - **Red-outlined triangles (`▲`, labeled `#1`, `#2`, ...)** are **pruned outlier bins** removed by the algorithm because their read count deviated sharply from the linear trend (for example, a repetitive element or a conserved gene attracting cross-mapped reads).
  - **Dashed gray line**: Initial linear fit across all 100 bins before pruning.
  - **Solid green/red line & shaded band**: Final linear fit and 95% confidence interval across the retained clean bins.
  - **Vertical error bars (whiskers)**: Show how evenly reads are spread *inside* each bin (from the 25th to the 75th percentile of CR coverage). Short whiskers mean reads are spread evenly across the CR segments inside that bin; tall whiskers mean a subset of CRs inside the bin has much higher coverage than the rest.
  - **Dot color (shared horizontal colorbar between Panels A & E)**: Shows whether that bin's weekly appearance over the 10 years matches the species' overall 10-year seasonal rhythm (**Cosine Similarity** from `0.0` in dark purple to `1.0` in bright yellow-green).
  - **Slim top strip**: Gray bars show how many distinct assembly contigs contributed to each bin; the blue line shows the median length (`kb`) of the individual CR segments inside that bin.

#### 2. Technical & Mathematical Specification
- **Why Variable-Size Linear Binning ($0.3\bar{L} \to 1.7\bar{L}$)**:
  - Partitioning total CR sequence $L_{\text{CR}}$ into equal-sized bins would collapse all $X_i$ values to a single constant ($\bar{L} = L_{\text{CR}}/100$), yielding zero variance in $X$ and making OLS regression impossible. Conversely, regressing per contig fails for chromosome-scale assemblies ($N < 40$ chromosomes) or highly fragmented drafts ($N > 50,000$ contigs).
  - Contigs containing CRs are sorted **shortest to longest** (deterministic tie-break by contig ID), and target capacities are spaced linearly across $i \in \{1, \dots, 100\}$:
    $$C_i = \bar{L} \cdot \left(0.3 + 1.4 \cdot \frac{i - 1}{99}\right), \quad \text{where } \bar{L} = \frac{L_{\text{CR}}}{100}, \quad \sum_{i=1}^{100} C_i = L_{\text{CR}}$$
    This creates a deterministic $5.67\times$ horizontal dynamic range on the X-axis while naturally separating small, repeat-enriched contigs (left, high contig count per bin in the top strip) from large chromosome scaffolds (right, 1–2 contigs per bin).
- **OLS Regression & 95% Confidence Band**:
  - For retained bins $\mathcal{R}$ ($n = |\mathcal{R}|$), ordinary least squares fits $\hat{Y}(x) = \hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1 x$. The two-sided 95% confidence band for the mean regression line at coordinate $x$ is:
    $$\hat{Y}(x) \pm t_{0.975,\, n-2} \cdot s_{Y|X} \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} + \frac{(x - \bar{X})^2}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}} (X_j - \bar{X})^2}}, \quad s_{Y|X} = \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}} (Y_j - \hat{Y}_j)^2}{n - 2}}$$
- **Length-Weighted Intra-Bin Coverage IQR Whiskers (`p25–p75`)**:
  - Within bin $b$, each CR segment $k$ has length $\ell_{b,k}$ and mapped read pairs $r_{b,k}$, giving per-base density $d_{b,k} = r_{b,k} / \ell_{b,k}$.
  - Because CR segments vary in length, unweighted quantiles would be dominated by short `400 bp` windows. We compute the **length-weighted 25th and 75th percentiles** ($q_{25}, q_{75}$) by sorting CR segments by $d_{b,k}$ and interpolating at cumulative length fractions $0.25\,X_b$ and $0.75\,X_b$, then scaling to the bin's total CR length:
    $$Y_{b,\text{p25}} = X_b \cdot q_{25}(d_b), \qquad Y_{b,\text{p75}} = X_b \cdot q_{75}(d_b)$$

### Panel B — Outlier Pruning Trajectory
*Subtitle: `Correlation gain vs. CR sequence retained per step`*

#### 1. Plain-English Guide
- **Core question**: *How many outlier bins had to be removed to reach the $R \ge 0.90$ validation target, and how much of the classifiable genome remained?*
- As the algorithm removes one outlier bin at each step (`Step 0, 1, 2, ...` on the X-axis):
  - The **blue curve (left Y-axis)** shows the linear correlation (**Pearson $R$**) climbing toward the **green dashed target line ($R = 0.90$)**.
  - The **orange curve (right Y-axis)** shows the percentage of the species' **Classifiable Region (CR) DNA remaining**, which must stay above the **red dotted floor ($50\%$)**.
- A steep jump in the blue curve during the first few steps means the noise was confined to just a few genomic bins; a flat blue curve that crosses the $50\%$ orange floor without reaching $R = 0.90$ indicates a systemic false positive (`FAILED_LOW_CORRELATION`).

#### 2. Technical & Mathematical Specification
- **Pre-Pruning Sanity Gate**:
  - Requires at least $50\%$ of bins to contain $\ge 1$ mapped read pair ($\text{Occupied Bins} \ge 50/100$) and positive initial OLS slope ($\hat{\beta}_1^{(0)} > 0$). Otherwise, the taxon immediately fails with `FAILED_LOW_OCCUPANCY`.
- **Bidirectional Recursive Vertical Residual Pruning**:
  - At iteration $t$, if $R^{(t)} \ge 0.90$ and $\hat{\beta}_1^{(t)} > 0$, pruning terminates (`VALIDATED_CLEAN` if $t=0$; `VALIDATED_PRUNED` if $t \ge 1$).
  - Otherwise, we identify the retained bin $i^*$ with the **maximum absolute vertical OLS residual**:
    $$i^* = \arg\max_{i \in \mathcal{R}^{(t)}} \left| Y_i - \left(\hat{\beta}_0^{(t)} + \hat{\beta}_1^{(t)} X_i\right) \right|$$
  - Why bidirectional ($|e_i|$ rather than only $e_i > 0$)? Both positive spikes (conserved repeats/cross-mapping) and extreme negative dropouts (assembly gaps, mis-assembled chimeric regions, or high-divergence haplotypes in large bins) distort the OLS slope and inflate residual variance.
  - Before removing $i^*$, we check whether $L_{\text{CR,retained}} - X_{i^*} \ge 0.50\,L_{\text{CR}}$. If removing $i^*$ would drop retained CR sequence below $50\%$, pruning halts and the species is classified as `FAILED_LOW_CORRELATION`.

### Panel C — Outlier Classification Quadrant
*Subtitle: `Excess read depth vs. temporal concordance (dot size = Gini inequality)`*

#### 1. Plain-English Guide
- **Core question**: *When a genomic bin has unexpectedly high read coverage, is it an **endogenous repeat** belonging to the target species itself, or a **foreign cross-mapping artifact** from a different organism?*
- Every bin (`100` total) is plotted along two diagnostic axes:
  1. **X-axis (`Excess Read Depth`)**: How many more (or fewer) reads the bin received compared to its length-proportional share of the clean classifiable genome ($\log_2$ fold-change relative to mean clean read density: `0` = exact match to clean genome average `Reads/kb`, `+1` = $2\times$ expected reads, `+2` = $4\times$, `+3` = $8\times$).
  2. **Y-axis (`Temporal Concordance / Cosine Similarity`)**: How closely the bin's 516-week time series matches the species' clean 516-week time series (`1.0` = identical weekly pattern; `0.0` = occurs in completely different weeks).
- This divides high-coverage outlier bins (`▲`) into two biologically distinct quadrants on the right:
  - **Upper-Right (`Endogenous Multi-Copy Repeat`)**: The bin has excess read depth ($>2\times$ expected), **but its reads appear in the exact same weeks as the rest of the species' genome** ($\text{Cosine Sim} \ge 0.60$). This happens when an uncollapsed tandem repeat, rRNA array, or transposable element from the target species itself is present in multiple copies in the organism's cells.
  - **Lower-Right (`Foreign Cross-Mapping Artifact`)**: The bin has excess read depth, **and its reads appear in different weeks from the target species** ($\text{Cosine Sim} < 0.60$). This happens when conserved sequences from a different organism (e.g., bacteria, fungi, or a distantly related abundant taxon blooming at another time of year) cross-map to a localized region of the reference assembly.
- **Dot size (`Gini Inequality`)**: Shows how unevenly reads are distributed *among the individual CR segments inside that bin*:
  - **Small dot (`Gini ≈ 0.20`)**: Reads are spread uniformly across many CRs inside the bin.
  - **Large dot (`Gini ≥ 0.85`, CR Hotspot)**: Almost all reads in the bin are concentrated into one or a few tiny CR segments.

#### 2. Technical & Mathematical Specification
- **Excess Read Depth ($\log_2[\text{Observed} / \text{Expected}]$ via Length-Proportional Clean Expectation)**:
  - Why length-proportional clean density instead of unconstrained OLS $\hat{Y}_b = \hat{\beta}_0 + \hat{\beta}_1 X_b$? In failed detections where a single small bin holds almost all reads (e.g., `bin_001`), an unconstrained OLS line has a negative slope ($\hat{\beta}_1 < 0$) that crosses zero and predicts negative expected reads for large bins; similarly, a negative Y-intercept ($\hat{\beta}_0 < 0$) under-predicts expected reads in the smallest bins.
  - Enforcing the physical boundary condition $E(X=0) = 0$, let $\bar{d}_{\mathcal{R}} = \frac{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}} Y_j}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}} X_j}$ be the mean read density (read pairs per bp) across retained clean bins $\mathcal{R}$. The length-proportional expected read count for bin $b$ of CR length $X_b$ is:
    $$E_b^{\text{prop}} = \max\!\left(1.0,\; \bar{d}_{\mathcal{R}} \cdot X_b\right) = \max\!\left(1.0,\; Y_{\text{clean, total}} \cdot \frac{X_b}{L_{\text{CR, clean}}}\right)$$
  - The horizontal coordinate in Panel C is then:
    $$\text{Excess Depth}_b = \log_2 \!\left( \max\!\left(0.125,\, \frac{Y_b + 1}{E_b^{\text{prop}} + 1}\right) \right)$$
- **516-Week Temporal Cosine Similarity ($\text{CosSim}_b$)**:
  - Let $\mathbf{v}_b = (v_{b,1}, \dots, v_{b,516})$ be the weekly read-pair vector of bin $b$, and $\mathbf{C} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{R}} \mathbf{v}_j$ be the aggregate weekly read-pair vector across all retained clean bins:
    $$\text{CosSim}_b = \frac{\mathbf{v}_b \cdot \mathbf{C}}{\|\mathbf{v}_b\|_2 \, \|\mathbf{C}\|_2} \in [0, 1]$$
- **Why Intra-Bin Length-Weighted Gini Coefficient ($G_b$) Instead of Top-1 CR Share**:
  - A simple "top-1 CR read fraction" fails when a bin contains `2` to `5` dominating CR segments (each holding ~20–30% of reads) or when CR lengths vary widely within a bin.
  - The **Length-Weighted Lorenz / Gini Coefficient ($G_b \in [0, 1]$)** measures inequality across *all* CR segments in bin $b$ relative to their sequence length shares. Sorting the $K_b$ CR segments in bin $b$ by ascending read density $d_{b,(1)} \le d_{b,(2)} \le \dots \le d_{b,(K_b)}$ with length shares $w_k = \ell_{b,(k)} / X_b$ and read shares $f_k = r_{b,(k)} / Y_b$, let $W_k = \sum_{j=1}^k w_j$ and $F_k = \sum_{j=1}^k f_j$ (with $W_0 = F_0 = 0$):
    $$G_b = 1 - \sum_{k=1}^{K_b} w_k \left(F_{k-1} + F_k\right)$$
  - $G_b = 0$ if and only if every CR segment in bin $b$ has identical read density ($r_{b,k} \propto \ell_{b,k}$); $G_b \to 1$ whether reads pile into `1` CR or `5` tiny hotspot CRs.

### Panels D1, D2 & D3 — Read Attrition, Sequence Identity Spectrum & Reference Sequence Funnel

#### 1. Plain-English Guide
- **Panel D1 (`eDNA Read Attrition` — left funnel)**:
  - Tracks what happens to all environmental DNA read pairs originally assigned to this species clade by Kraken 2 across **5 sequential quality filters** (with each horizontal bar's width strictly proportional to the percentage of read pairs retained):
    1. **`1. Raw Kraken Clade Pairs`**: Total read pairs classified to the species clade by Kraken 2 across all 516 weekly samples.
    2. **`2. CR Mapped (MAPQ ≥ 30)`**: Read pairs that align uniquely and with high confidence (`MAPQ ≥ 30`) to the species' Classifiable Regions (`minimap2`).
    3. **`3. Passed ≥ 80% Aligned`**: Read pairs where at least $80\%$ of the sequencing read aligns without large soft-clipped overhangs.
    4. **`4. Passed ≥ 99% Identity`**: Read pairs with at least $99\%$ base-pair identity ($\le 1\%$ mismatches/indels) to the reference genome.
    5. **`5. Clean Validated Bins`**: High-identity read pairs that land inside the retained **clean genomic bins** after outlier pruning.
- **Panel D2 (`Alignment Identity Spectrum` — top-right bar chart)**:
  - Shows the sequence similarity (`% Identity`, from `<90%` up to `100%`) of reads that aligned to the Classifiable Regions (Stage 3 of the funnel):
    - **Red bars (`<90%` through `98%`, left of the dashed cutoff line)**: Reads rejected because they have $>1\%$ sequence divergence—often caused by cross-mapping from a related sister species (which typically peaks around `94%–98%` identity).
    - **Green bars (`99%` and `100%`, right of the cutoff line)**: Reads passing the $\ge 99\%$ identity threshold in **retained clean bins**.
    - **Amber/orange stacked bars (`99%` and `100%`)**: Reads passing $\ge 99\%$ identity that landed in **pruned outlier bins**.
- **Panel D3 (`Reference Sequence Funnel` — bottom-right funnel)**:
  - Shows how much of the reference genome sequence is classifiable and retained (with each horizontal bar's width strictly proportional to its `% of genome`):
    1. **`1. Total Genome Assembly`**: Total base pairs in the reference genome (`Mb`, `100.0%`).
    2. **`2. Classifiable Regions (CR)`**: How much of that genome can actually be classified to the species clade by Kraken 2 (`Mb` and `% of genome`). Repeat-rich genomes (such as birds or amphibians) or species with very close database relatives may have a low CR percentage (`10–30%`), whereas unique genomes have a high CR percentage (`70–95%`).
    3. **`3. Clean CRs (Post-Pruning)`**: How much classifiable DNA remains after removing outlier bins (`Mb`, `% of genome`, and `% of CR`).

#### 2. Technical & Mathematical Specification
- **Alignment Filtering Criteria (`Stage 4` / `filter_sam_to_parquet.py`)**:
  - For every primary (`!0x904`), mapped (`!0x4`) SAM alignment with `MAPQ >= 30`, let $L_{\text{read}}$ be the query length (`len(SEQ)` or CIGAR query length), $S_{\text{clip}}$ be total soft-clipped bases (`S` CIGAR ops), $L_{\text{aln}} = L_{\text{read}} - S_{\text{clip}}$ be the aligned query span, and $\text{NM}$ be the edit distance tag (`NM:i`):
    $$\text{Aligned Fraction} = \frac{L_{\text{aln}}}{L_{\text{read}}} \ge 0.80, \qquad \text{Mismatch Fraction} = \frac{\text{NM}}{L_{\text{aln}}} \le 0.01 \;\;(\ge 99\%\text{ identity})$$
- **How the `<99%` Identity Histogram in D2 is Computed Without Re-Mapping**:
  - During the single streaming pass of `minimap2` into `filter_sam_to_parquet.py`, every alignment passing `MAPQ >= 30` and $L_{\text{aln}} / L_{\text{read}} \ge 0.80$ has its exact integer identity bin:
    $$b_{\text{id}} = \max\!\left(89,\, \min\!\left(100,\, \left\lfloor 100 \cdot \left(1 - \frac{\text{NM}}{L_{\text{aln}}}\right) \right\rfloor \right)\right)$$
    tallied in an O(1) in-memory array (`lt99_id_hist_records[89..98]`) and written to `{t_id}.identity_histogram_lt99.tsv`, while the `99%` ($\text{NM} > 0$) and `100%` ($\text{NM} = 0$) passing alignments are stored directly in `{t_id}.passing_alignments.parquet` and split into clean vs. pruned bins.

### Panel E — Genome-Wide CR Coverage Map (Manhattan Plot)
*Subtitle: `Read density across N CR segments (rose bands = pruned bins; gray band = linear 0–1 scale)`*

#### 1. Plain-English Guide
- **Core question**: *What does read coverage look like across every single Classifiable Region (CR) segment in the genome, rather than just summarized into 100 bins?*
- Each dot is one individual **CR segment** (typically tens of thousands across a eukaryotic genome), plotted from left to right along cumulative classifiable genome coordinates (`bin_001` $\rightarrow$ `bin_100`, in Megabases).
- The Y-axis shows **Read Density (`Reads / kb`)**:
  - Because some CRs have `0` reads while repeat hotspots can have `1,000+ Reads / kb`, the Y-axis uses a **hybrid linear-logarithmic scale**:
    - Inside the **shaded gray band at the bottom (`0` to `1 Read / kb`)**, the scale is **linear** so zero-coverage and low-coverage CRs remain clearly visible.
    - Above the **dashed line at `y = 1`**, the scale is **logarithmic ($\log_{10}$)** (`10`, `100`, `1,000 Reads / kb`) so extreme spikes do not crush the rest of the genome flat.
- **Vertical rose/pink bands** mark the genomic spans of **pruned outlier bins**, allowing you to see immediately whether a pruned bin was driven by a single massive CR spike or a broader cluster of high-coverage segments.
- Dot colors use the **same horizontal Cosine Similarity colorbar** located right above Panel E.

#### 2. Technical & Mathematical Specification
- **CR Segment Coordinates & Density**:
  - Each CR segment $k$ on contig $c$ (assigned to bin $b$) has start/end coordinates $[s_k, e_k)$, length $\ell_k = e_k - s_k$, and cumulative midpoint coordinate $m_k = \left(\sum_{j < k} \ell_j + \frac{1}{2}\ell_k\right) / 10^6\text{ Mb}$.
  - Read density is $D_k = \frac{1000 \cdot r_k}{\ell_k}\text{ (read pairs per kb)}$.
- **Symmetrical Logarithmic Transformation (`symlog`, `linthresh = 1.0`)**:
  - Standard log axes cannot plot $D_k = 0$, while ad-hoc $\log_{10}(D_k + \epsilon)$ distorts small values depending on $\epsilon$. Using `symlog` with `linthresh = 1.0` maps $y \in [0, 1]$ linearly and $y > 1$ as $1 + \log_{10}(y)$, explicitly demarcated by the shaded gray band and dashed $y = 1$ boundary.
- **Equal Horizontal Padding**:
  - Left and right X-axis limits are padded symmetrically by $\pm 2.8\%$ of total CR span (`[-pad_mb, max_mb + pad_mb]`) so boundary dots at `bin_001` and `bin_100` are never clipped by the axis spines.

### Panel F — Weekly Signal Retention
*Subtitle: `Clean vs. raw counts across 516 weeks`*

#### 1. Plain-English Guide
- **Core question**: *Does the strict validation filter change which weeks the species is detected, or does it simply scale the weekly counts down by a consistent factor?*
- Each dot is one of the **516 weekly air-filter samples**:
  - **X-axis**: Raw Kraken 2 clade read pairs in that week ($\log_{10}(\text{reads}+1)$ scale).
  - **Y-axis**: Validated clean-bin read pairs in that week ($\log_{10}(\text{reads}+1)$ scale).
- **Dashed diagonal line (`Y = X`)**: Where dots would fall if $100\%$ of raw Kraken reads survived validation.
- **Solid dark curve (`Y = c · X`)**: Expected curve when a constant fraction $c$ of raw reads is retained every week.
- If all dots cluster tightly along the solid curve (`R_log ≈ 0.95–1.00` and `Lin R ≈ 0.99–1.00`), the raw Kraken time series had the right temporal shape all along. If there are dots far to the right along the bottom axis (`Y = 0` despite high `X`), those weeks were false-positive spikes completely eliminated by validation!

#### 2. Technical & Mathematical Specification
- **Why Three Complementary Correlation Metrics ($R_{\text{lin}}$, $R_{\log}$, and Spearman $\rho$) Are Reported**:
  1. **Linear Pearson $R$ (`Lin R`)**: Computed on untransformed weekly counts $(x_t, y_t)$. Heavily weighted toward peak bloom weeks (e.g., 1–3 summer weeks with $10^5$–$10^6$ reads).
  2. **Log-Space Pearson $R_{\log}$ (`R_log`)**: Computed on $(\log_{10}(x_t + 1),\, \log_{10}(y_t + 1))$ across all 516 weeks:
     $$R_{\log} = \text{corr}\!\left(\log_{10}(x_t + 1),\; \log_{10}(y_t + 1)\right)$$
     Because Panel F is plotted on $\log_{10}(v+1)$ axes, $R_{\log}$ measures the exact visual linearity across all orders of magnitude ($10^0 \to 10^7$), preventing 2 massive bloom weeks from masking low-abundance off-season discordance.
  3. **Spearman Rank Correlation ($\rho$)**: Non-parametric rank correlation across all 516 weeks.
- **Proportional Retention Curve on $\log_{10}(v+1)$ Axes**:
  - Let $c = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{516} y_t}{\sum_{t=1}^{516} x_t}$ be the global clean-to-raw read retention ratio. On $\tilde{x} = \log_{10}(x+1)$ coordinates, a linear proportionality $y = c \cdot x$ maps to the exact curve $\tilde{y}(\tilde{x}) = \log_{10}\!\left(c \cdot (10^{\tilde{x}} - 1) + 1\right)$.

### Panel G — Bin Temporal Occupancy
*Subtitle: `Clean median: X% (K/516 wks) | Effective active weeks (N_eff) = ...`*

#### 1. Plain-English Guide
- **Core question**: *In how many of the 516 weekly samples does each genomic bin appear, and is the species detected often enough for Temporal Cosine Similarity to be meaningful?*
- Each dot is one of the 100 genomic bins:
  - **X-axis (`Weekly Sample Occupancy %`)**: Percentage of the 516 weekly samples where that bin has at least 1 mapped read pair (a histogram at the top shows the distribution across all 100 bins, with a dashed vertical line at the clean-bin median).
  - **Y-axis (`Temporal Concordance / Cosine Similarity`)**: How closely the bin's weekly pattern matches the species' overall time series.
- **Why this panel is essential**:
  - **Cosine Similarity can become degenerate in two opposite regimes**:
    1. **Extreme Episodic / Single-Spike Regime ($N_{\text{eff}} \approx 1\text{–}3\text{ weeks}$)**: When a species (such as the butterfly *Pieris napi* or *Pieris melete*) has $>90\%$ of all its 10-year reads in just `2` or `3` summer bloom weeks, *any* bin that catches reads during those 2 weeks automatically gets $\text{Cosine Sim} \approx 0.99$! Panel G immediately alerts you to this by showing **low sample occupancy (`<2%`)** and a tiny **Effective Active Weeks (`N_eff = 2.3w`)** in the subtitle.
    2. **Ultra-Sparse / Low-Abundance Regime**: When a species is present at very low read depth across many weeks (e.g., 1–2 reads per week), each individual bin only catches a read in a random handful of weeks (`Poisson dropout`), artificially lowering $\text{Cosine Sim}$ (`0.40–0.75`) even for completely clean bins. Panel G reveals this as a rising curve from low occupancy/lower cosine on the left to higher occupancy/higher cosine on the right.

#### 2. Technical & Mathematical Specification
- **Bin Sample Occupancy ($\text{Occ}_b$)**:
  $$\text{Occ}_b = \frac{100}{516} \sum_{t=1}^{516} \mathbb{I}(v_{b,t} \ge 1)$$
- **Effective Number of Active Weeks ($N_{\text{eff}}$, Inverse Simpson Diversity of the Time Series)**:
  - Let $p_t = \frac{C_t}{\sum_{u=1}^{516} C_u}$ be the fraction of total clean reads occurring in week $t$. The **Effective Number of Active Weeks** is the inverse Simpson concentration index:
    $$N_{\text{eff}} = \frac{1}{\sum_{t=1}^{516} p_t^2} = \frac{\left(\sum_{t=1}^{516} C_t\right)^2}{\sum_{t=1}^{516} C_t^2} \in [1,\, 516]$$
  - If all reads occur in a single week, $N_{\text{eff}} = 1.0\text{w}$; if reads are spread evenly across $K$ weeks, $N_{\text{eff}} = K\text{w}$. Whenever $N_{\text{eff}} \le 3\text{w}$, $\text{CosSim}_b$ has low discriminative power because the 516-dimensional inner product collapses onto 1–3 coordinates.

### Panel I — 10-Year Seasonal Phenology Profile
*Subtitle: `Multi-year average cycle across weeks 1–52 (pre- vs. post-validation concordance r = ...)`*

#### 1. Plain-English Guide
- **Core question**: *What is the species' typical seasonal cycle (January to December) averaged over the 10 years, and did validation remove off-season false-positive noise?*
- By summing each calendar week (`Week 1` to `Week 52`) across all 10 years and applying a gentle 3-week smoothing filter:
  - **Dashed gray curve (`Pre-Validation Raw`)**: Seasonal pattern of raw Kraken 2 counts (expressed on the **left Y-axis** as the `% of the annual total signal occurring in each week`).
  - **Solid green/red curve & shaded area (`Post-Validation Clean`)**: Seasonal pattern after validation.
  - **Pink shaded gaps (`Purged Off-Season / Cross-Mapping Noise`)**: Weeks of the year where raw Kraken 2 showed a higher share of reads than the validated signal—revealing off-season false-positive spikes that were successfully removed!
  - **Right Y-axis (`Mean Clean Pairs / Week`)**: Shows the actual average number of clean read pairs per week during that time of year across the 10-year dataset.

#### 2. Technical & Mathematical Specification
- **Circular 3-Week Moving Average Across the Dec–Jan Boundary**:
  - For each calendar week $w \in \{1, \dots, 52\}$, let $S_w = \sum_{y=2013}^{2022} C_{y,w}$ be the 10-year summed read count. To reduce single-week sampling stochasticity without shifting seasonal peak timing or creating edge artifacts between Week 52 (late December) and Week 1 (early January), we apply a symmetric `[0.25, 0.50, 0.25]` **circular convolution** on the 52-week ring:
    $$\tilde{S}_w = 0.25\, S_{((w-2)\bmod 52)+1} + 0.50\, S_w + 0.25\, S_{(w\bmod 52)+1}$$
- **Dual Normalization (Relative Annual Share vs. Absolute Mean Weekly Abundance)**:
  - Left Y-axis plots relative phenological share $P_w = 100 \cdot \frac{\tilde{S}_w}{\sum_{k=1}^{52} \tilde{S}_k}\;(\%/\text{week})$ so pre- and post-validation seasonal shapes can be directly compared even when total read counts differ by $2\times$–$10\times$.
  - Right Y-axis scales the exact same post-validation curve to $\bar{C}_w^{\text{clean}} = \tilde{S}_w^{\text{clean}} / N_{\text{years}}$ (mean clean read pairs per week).
  - Phenology concordance $r$ is the Pearson correlation between $P_w^{\text{raw}}$ and $P_w^{\text{clean}}$ across $w = 1, \dots, 52$.

### Panel J — Bin × Week Hierarchical Clustermap
*Subtitle: `100 CR bins clustered by temporal profile across M active weeks (Ward linkage)`*

#### 1. Plain-English Guide
- **Core question**: *Do all 100 genomic bins light up together in the exact same weeks across the 10-year time series, or do certain bins behave like a separate organism?*
- **Rows (`100` rows)**: The 100 genomic CR bins, grouped automatically by a **hierarchical clustering tree (dendrogram on the left)** so bins with similar weekly patterns sit next to each other.
- **Columns (`Active Weeks`)**: The monitoring weeks (ordered chronologically from 2013 to 2022) in which the species was detected.
- **Heatmap colors (`Read Density` — right colorbar at bottom)**: Darker blue means higher read coverage (`Reads / kb` on a $\log_{10}$ scale) in that bin during that week; pale yellow means zero reads.
  - **What a true biological detection looks like**: Vertical stripes that run **continuously from top to bottom across all 100 rows**, showing that the entire genome appears and disappears in lockstep during the same weeks.
  - **What a cross-mapping artifact looks like**: A horizontal block of rows (marked in **red** in the `Status` strip) that lights up in weeks where the rest of the genome is dark, or stays dark when the rest of the genome is active.
- **Three Vertical Annotation Strips (between the dendrogram and heatmap, labeled at the bottom)**:
  1. **`CosSim`**: 516-week Temporal Cosine Similarity (`Viridis`, shared with Panels A & E).
  2. **`Occ%`**: Bin Sample Occupancy across all 516 weeks (`PuBu` blue gradient, with its own **`Occupancy (% of 516 Wks)` colorbar** at the bottom-left of Panel J).
  3. **`Status`**: Whether the bin was **Retained** (green) or **Pruned** (red) by the regression algorithm.

#### 2. Technical & Mathematical Specification
- **Why Length-Normalized Log Read Density $M_{b,t} = \log_{10}(\text{Reads/kb}_{b,t} + 1)$**:
  - Because bin CR lengths $X_b$ vary by $5.67\times$ ($0.3\bar{L} \to 1.7\bar{L}$), raw read counts would cluster large bins (`bin_100`) separately from small bins (`bin_001`) purely due to bin size. Dividing by bin CR length in kilobases ($X_b / 1000$) puts all 100 bins on an identical per-kb coverage footing, and $\log_{10}(\cdot + 1)$ stabilizes variance across orders of magnitude.
- **What "Active Weeks" Are**:
  - Over 516 weeks, many seasonal species are absent in winter (`0` reads across all 100 bins). Including hundreds of all-zero columns would compress informative weeks into sub-pixel slivers and contribute zero Euclidean distance ($0 - 0 = 0$).
  - We select all weeks with $>0$ total mapped reads across the 100 bins; if more than `65` weeks are non-zero, we select the **top 65 weeks by total mapped read count** and sort them in strict chronological order (`2013` $\rightarrow$ `2022`).
- **Why Euclidean Distance + Ward's Minimum-Variance Linkage + Optimal Leaf Ordering**:
  - **Why not correlation or cosine distance for clustering rows?** Correlation/cosine distance is scale-invariant: a noisy bin with `0.1 Reads/kb` and a massive rRNA spike bin with `5,000 Reads/kb` in the same week would have distance `0.0` and cluster together! **Euclidean distance on $\log_{10}(\text{Reads/kb} + 1)$** jointly preserves both **coverage magnitude** (separating high-depth repeat bins from normal single-copy bins) and **temporal co-occurrence** (separating discordant cross-mapping bins that fire in off-season weeks).
  - **Why Ward's Minimum-Variance Linkage (`method="ward"`)?** Single linkage suffers from chaining, while average/complete linkage can produce unbalanced singleton splinters on noisy metagenomic profiles. Ward's method minimizes total within-cluster sum of squares (equivalent to hierarchical $k$-means variance partitioning in Euclidean space), cleanly partitioning the 100 bins into coherent blocks (core single-copy genome vs. high-depth endogenous repeats vs. discordant artifacts).
  - **Bar-Joseph Optimal Leaf Ordering (`optimal_ordering=True`)**: Flips internal dendrogram nodes to minimize the sum of distances between adjacent rows without altering tree topology, ensuring smooth vertical visual gradients.
